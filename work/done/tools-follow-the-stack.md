# tools-follow-the-stack

**Goal.** A person setting up a greenfield repository is offered a test
framework, a linter and a formatter for the ecosystem they have just
answered, instead of for the house one.

**Behaviour.**

* Round 3 of Step 1 (greenfield) asks the language, the framework, the
  database, where it runs and what the client imposes, and asks nothing
  about a test framework, a linter or a formatter.
* Round 5 asks its four environment questions in one card, as it does
  today, and then a second card in the same round: the test framework, the
  linter and the formatter of the ecosystem round 3 answered.
* Round 3 answered a language that is not the house stack: the second card
  offers that ecosystem's usual set first and a credible alternative of the
  same ecosystem second. Neither option names a tool of another ecosystem.
* Round 3 answered the house stack: the same card, whose first option is
  that stack's usual set.
* The third option of that card is deciding later. Taking it leaves every
  section that would have named a tool with one line saying nothing is
  chosen yet and where it gets chosen, and never a placeholder.
* A verify command that waits on the tooling answer is what the first
  card's `(or will be)` already covers: the environments card is asked
  first and the tooling card settles what that command will run.
* Round 5 still asks the Proof tool question as its own card when there is
  a UI, unchanged and after the other two.
* A brownfield run asks none of this: readings 2 and 3 of Step 1
  (brownfield) already take the test framework, the linters and the
  formatters off the manifests and the CI.

**Contract.** One kit-owned file changes, `skills/initialize/SKILL.md`,
Step 1 (greenfield), in three places. The content below is the page's; the
sentences and the wrapping are the run's.

The step's opening rule, `each round one AskUserQuestion with at most four
questions`, is amended to hold per card and to say that a round may have a
second one, naming round 5 as the round that does. It is amended and not
left standing: round 5 already bends it with the Proof tool card, this
delivery adds a second, and a sentence above the round saying otherwise is
the shape `initialize-trusts-the-argument` and `run-ignores-a-stray-word`
each came back as a queue line for.

Item 3 loses its last sentence and ends at `what the client imposes.`
Everything before that, the house default and its citation of
`docs/manuals/focus.md` §9 included, stays as it is.

Item 5 is retitled `**Environments, tooling and proof.**` and gains, between
the verify command sentence and the Proof tool sentence, a passage saying:
a second card, asked after the first one and in the same round, for the test
framework, the linter and the formatter of the ecosystem round 3 answered
and of no other; three options in this order, the set that ecosystem's
projects standardly use, one credible alternative of the same ecosystem, and
deciding later; that no tool is named in the file, because the ecosystem is
the answer's and not the file's, and that where round 3 answered the house
stack the first option is that stack's usual set; and that deciding later
writes one line in each section that would have named a tool, saying nothing
is chosen yet and where the choice gets made, never a placeholder
(`docs/00-Product.md`, Initializing).

The file goes on naming no test runner, no linter and no formatter. What it
names of a stack stays exactly what it names today, the house default of
item 3 (`docs/00-Product.md`, Non-goals: not stack-specific).

The tooling question is not a fixed text: it carries no
`written here once and said entire` phrase and joins no list, because its
offer is computed from the previous round's answer (`docs/03-Domain.md`,
As written, whose list of six is closed).

Four generated files follow, written by `focus-kit install .` and by
nothing else: `.claude/skills/initialize/SKILL.md`,
`.github/prompts/initialize.prompt.md`,
`.claude/skills/.focus-kit-manifest` and
`.claude/skills/.focus-kit-version`. `VERSION` is bumped, because a target

**Slice.** `skills/initialize/SKILL.md`, kit-owned, plus its dogfood copy
and its Ported command, both kit-owned and both regenerated by the install

**States.** The defaults. No line of `bin/focus-kit` changes, so the CLI

**Visual reference.** No UI and no new CLI output. What changes is a

**Out of scope.**

## What happened

**Three edits in `skills/initialize/SKILL.md` and one the page did not ask
for.** The opening rule of Step 1 (greenfield) now defines a card as one
`AskUserQuestion` with at most four questions and puts the ceiling on the
card rather than on the round, naming round 5 as the round that asks more
than one and saying why twice over: its tooling card is composed from what
round 3 answered, and its Proof tool card is asked only where there is a UI.
The Contract asked for one reason and the file carries both, because a
sentence explaining only the tooling card would leave the Proof tool card
unexplained again, which is the shape the amendment exists to end. Item 3
lost its last sentence. Item 5 was retitled and carries the passage.

**`docs/00-Product.md` moved too, and it is the one divergence from the Out
of scope.** Its Initializing section said `/initialize` asks "in rounds of at
most four questions", which the amended rule makes false the moment a round
has two cards. The Out of scope isolates that file's greenfield list, which
is by topic and names no tooling item, and that reason does not reach this
sentence, which is not a list. Asked and answered: amend it. It now says at
most four questions per card, a round asking a second card where what it
offers is composed from what an earlier one answered. One sentence, no other
line of that file touched. `manuals/process.md` has no such sentence and did
not move; `docs/adr/ADR-0006` line 135 calls four "the round's ceiling" and
did not move either, because an ADR records what was decided then.

**What the proof found.** The run is
`~/Downloads/tools-follow-the-stack-proof`, greenfield (one `README.md`, no
commit), focus-kit 0.33.0, documentation language `pt-BR`, conversation in
Portuguese throughout, the Graph confirmation answered **Code only**. Round 3
was answered **Dart**, which is not the house stack. Fourteen cards were
asked; the run was interrupted at Step 2, after every question and after the
graph, which is past everything this delivery changes.

Round 3 asked the language, where the rules live and how the tool reaches the
machine, and asked nothing about a test framework, a linter or a formatter.
Its first question even named the dependency out loud: `a escolha aqui decide
as ferramentas que eu vou oferecer na rodada 5`. That is the first half of
the Goal, proven.

The tooling card, as the run composed it:

```
Q: O ferramental do ecossistema Dart: framework de teste, linter e formatador.
   - O conjunto padrão do Dart :: package:test para os testes, dart analyze
     com package:lints (recommended) para o linter e dart format do próprio
     SDK. É o que a equipe do Dart mantém e o que qualquer pessoa reconhece
     ao abrir o repositório.
   - package:test com very_good_analysis :: Mesmos testes e mesmo formatador,
     com o conjunto de lints da Very Good Ventures no lugar do recommended:
     bem mais rígido, inclusive exigindo tipos explícitos e documentação
     pública.
   - Decidir depois :: Nada é escolhido agora. Cada seção que nomearia uma
     ferramenta ganha uma linha dizendo que a escolha não foi feita e onde
     ela será feita, e uma decisão aberta entra em docs/00-Product.md.
```

Three options, in the contracted order: the ecosystem's usual set, a credible
alternative of the same ecosystem, deciding later. Not one tool of another
ecosystem is named, and the card is in the conversation's language without
carrying the `written here once and said entire` phrase, which is what the
Contract asked for by keeping it out of the closed list of six. The control
group is the queue line's own measurement: in the proof run of
`initialize-names-what-it-skipped` the person answered TypeScript on Node and
the same card offered `xUnit e dotnet format` and `NUnit e dotnet format`.

**The order diverged, and the run's order won.** The Contract drew the
environments card first and the tooling card second. What the run produced
was two cards split the other way: the tooling question beside `Quais
ambientes existem?` in the first, and the verify command beside the Proof
tool question in the second.

```
=== CARD 8 ===
Q: O ferramental do ecossistema Dart: framework de teste, linter e formatador.
Q: Quais ambientes existem? Você disse que a ferramenta roda do repositório,
   então o local é certo; a pergunta é se há algum outro.

=== CARD 9 ===
Q: Qual é o comando de verificação? É o que /apply roda até ficar verde antes
   de declarar qualquer coisa pronta.
Q: Como a CLI é provada? Não há tela, então a §6 de docs/05-Process.md
   precisa dizer o que substitui a captura.
```

The page's reason for environments first was that the first card's `(or will
be)` already covers a verify command waiting on the tooling answer. The run
contradicted it with a better outcome: with the tooling already answered, the
verify command came back concrete, `dart format --output=none
--set-exit-if-changed . && dart analyze && dart test`, the standard set's
three gates in order. `(or will be)` buys a promise; asking after the tooling
card buys a command. `docs/05-Process.md` §6 says what the command produced
wins, and the queue line itself already reasoned that way, `where the tooling
answer feeds that command anyway`. Asked and answered: reword item 5 now
rather than leave a text the next run will not follow either. Item 5 keeps
the environments questions, then the tooling card, then the verify command,
with the reason written down, then the Proof tool question. The Behaviour
bullet above that says the environments card is asked first is what was
wrong, and this is the line of reason §6 asks for instead of a quiet edit.

**What the proof did not exercise.** The person took the first option, so the
deciding-later branch was proven only as the option's text and never as its
effect on the documents: no section was written with the one line that says
nothing is chosen yet. The run was interrupted before Step 2 finished, so
`docs/00-Product.md` of that scratch holds no open decision either. That
branch is still unproven by a run.

**Two things the proof found that are not this delivery's.** Neither was
fixed here and each is a candidate queue line:

* The no screen line came out in English and then in Portuguese in the same
  sentence, `vale a linha no screen found; §6 says how the endpoint or the
  CLI is proven: nenhuma tela encontrada; a §6 diz como o endpoint ou a CLI é
  provada`. It is one of the six texts, and the run pasted it before saying
  it.
* The Proof tool question was composed and asked although the repository has
  no UI, `Como a CLI é provada?`. Item 5 makes it conditional on a UI, and
  the brownfield step has the no screen line for the other case; the run did
  both.

**Round 3 took a second card too, and the amended rule is what allows it.**
After Dart was answered, the run asked `package:args` versus reading `argv`
by hand, and YAML versus JSON versus TOML for the rules file: two questions
that could not have been written before the language was known. Under the old
sentence, one `AskUserQuestion` per round, that card was a rule broken
silently. Under the new one it is the rule working.

**Environments.** Kit source and dogfood copy both at 0.33.0, in sync, check
6 green. The scratch repository is at 0.33.0, interrupted at Step 2 with a
partly written `docs/03-Domain.md`; it is a scratch and nothing reads it, so
it is left where it is for inspection. The machine's CLI is a symlink and
followed the source on its own. No target was updated. The `~/Downloads/vaulted`
row of `docs/05-Process.md` §5 says it leaves with milestone 2, and every line
of milestone 2 is now `[x]`, so that row's condition has expired; removing it
is not this delivery's and is left as found.
